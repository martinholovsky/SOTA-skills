# Comment triage against AACR-Bench — a null on accuracy, and a threshold shift underneath it

**2026-09-12** · `evals/run-comment-triage.py --samples 3 --temp 0.7` ·
`claude-sonnet-4.6` · 40 cases × 2 arms × 3 samples · objective verdict parsing, no judge ·
pre-registered in [PRE-REGISTRATION-COMMENT-TRIAGE.md](PRE-REGISTRATION-COMMENT-TRIAGE.md)
**before any call**, and committed before the result existed.

Instrument: **AACR-Bench** (Alibaba Aone, Apache-2.0), adopted because this library's own
audit-precision instruments saturate.

## Result

| arm | accuracy | recall on REAL comments | recall on NOT_REAL comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare | 0.517 | 0.500 | 0.533 |
| with `rules/03` §2+§4 | 0.542 | **0.317** | **0.767** |
| **delta** | **+0.025** | **−0.183** | **+0.234** |

Chance is exactly 0.500 (the set is balanced 20/20 by construction). **Zero parse
failures** in 240 calls.

## The registered readings, both of them

**On the primary metric: NULL.** +0.025 falls inside the registered null band (|Δ| < 0.03).

**The registered trap fired, and it is the real finding.** The pre-registration said a lift
could not be claimed if the with-arm lost more than **0.05** of label-1 recall. It lost
**0.183** — nearly four times the limit. So even had accuracy cleared +0.05, the registered
conclusion was already written: **the guidance moves the decision threshold, it does not
improve discrimination.**

What the guidance actually did is legible in the two recalls moving in opposite directions
by similar magnitudes: **+0.234** on rejecting bad comments, **−0.183** on accepting good
ones. That is a more skeptical reviewer, not a better one.

**Writing the trap down in advance is what makes this readable.** Accuracy alone said
"+0.025, a small positive". On an unbalanced set — the source's real 70/30 mix — the same
behaviour would have printed as a *loss*. Same model, same guidance, three different
headline numbers depending on choices made before the run.

## The instrument did its job, which was the point of adopting it

Void condition 1 was *no saturation*, and neither arm came close: **0.517 and 0.542, both
barely above chance.** Compare the measure this replaces — 30 claims, **1.00 in both
arms**, from which the audit-accuracy axis was closed at +0.00 across nine instruments.

Those two readings are not in conflict; they are the same capability through instruments of
different difficulty, and **ours could not tell**. The honest restatement is that we never
had evidence of audit precision — we had a measure too easy to discriminate. This one
discriminates, and the absolute number is humbling: on expert-labelled review comments,
roughly half of whose negatives are plausible model output, a current flagship is **near
chance either way**.

## Exploratory, NOT registered — read as a hypothesis

Splitting by whether the comment was AI-authored (a field carried from the source set) is a
post-hoc cut and is reported as one:

| subset | n | bare | with |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI-authored comments | 28 | 0.536 | 0.607 |
| human-authored comments | 12 | 0.472 | **0.389** |

The suggestion is that skepticism catches machine-generated plausible-sounding review noise
and wrongly rejects terse human expert comments. **n=12 on the human half — this is a
question to test, not a result.**

## What this does and does not say about `rules/03` §4

It does **not** refute §4's *"default the verdict to REFUTED when the evidence is
ambiguous"*. That instruction governs **findings you produced yourself**, where a false
positive costs a team's time and an over-eager auditor is the common failure. This eval is
an adjacent but different task — judging **someone else's** comment — and the asymmetry
that justifies the default does not obviously transfer.

What it does establish is that the instruction has a **measurable price**, which the rule
does not currently state: on a balanced set it trades ~0.18 of true-finding recall for
~0.23 of false-finding rejection. Whether that is a good trade depends on a base rate and a
cost asymmetry that the rule leaves unstated. **Left as an open question rather than a rule
change — one model, one day, 40 of 2,145 records is not enough to re-tune a rule on.**

## What this cannot establish

- One model, one day, **40 of 2,145** records, at a 28/12 AI/human comment mix.
- It measures **triage** — the refutation half of an audit — not defect **discovery**.
- The negatives include comments generated by models; `source_model` is retained in the
  case file so any contamination is visible rather than assumed absent.
